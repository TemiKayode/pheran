What Does a Bespoke Evening Gown Cost in Lagos?

Bespoke pricing can feel opaque from the outside — quotes that vary wildly designer to designer, with little explanation of why. Here's an honest breakdown of what actually drives the cost.

PHERAN Style Guides·6 min read

What You're Actually Paying For

A bespoke gown's price isn't one number — it's the sum of a few distinct costs stacked together: the base construction (pattern-making, cutting, sewing to your measurements), the fabric itself, and any embellishment or hand-work like beading or embroidery. Understanding each piece separately makes it much easier to compare quotes from different designers, or to know where you can adjust to fit a budget.

Base Construction Pricing

This covers the actual craftsmanship — pattern-drafting to your exact measurements, cutting, and construction. It varies by garment complexity more than almost anything else:

Evening gowns sit at the top because they typically involve more fabric, more structural tailoring (boning, lining, internal support), and more finishing time than a simpler daywear piece.

Fabric Is Where Quotes Diverge Most

This is usually the biggest source of price variation between designers, because fabric cost varies enormously by type — and some designers absorb more of that cost into a flat quote than others, which is part of why "bespoke gown" prices can range so widely with no clear explanation.

  • Cotton — the most accessible fabric premium, adding ₦2,000
  • Linen — breathable and increasingly popular for daywear pieces, around ₦3,000
  • Ankara print — adds roughly ₦4,000, price driven by print quality and origin
  • Wool blend — for structured tailoring, around ₦7,000
  • Silk blend — a common evening-gown choice, typically ₦8,000+
  • Aso-oke — the highest premium, often ₦12,000+, reflecting the hand-loom process (see our full aso-oke guide)

Rush Delivery Changes the Math

Standard bespoke turnaround is 10–14 business days. If your event is closer than that, rush delivery (5–7 business days) is available — but it costs more, typically an additional 40% on top of your garment and fabric total, since it means prioritising your piece ahead of the standard queue. Building in a two-week buffer before your event, where possible, is the simplest way to avoid this cost entirely.

Ready-to-Wear vs. Bespoke: The Real Trade-off

Our ready-to-wear evening pieces — like the hand-embellished gowns in our current edit — are priced higher up front than a base bespoke quote, because the embellishment work (in the case of our showpiece gown, over 800 hand-placed beads) is already built into the price of a finished piece. Bespoke starts lower because you're pricing the base construction and fabric separately, then adding embellishment as you choose it. Neither is "cheaper" outright — it depends on how much customisation and hand-work you actually want.
