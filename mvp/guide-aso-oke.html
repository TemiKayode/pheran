Custom Aso-Oke Dresses in Nigeria: The Complete Guide

Aso-oke is one of the most demanding fabrics to design for — and one of the most rewarding to get right. Here's what to actually know before you commission a piece, from weave types to what "custom" really changes.

PHERAN Style Guides·8 min read

What Aso-Oke Actually Is

Aso-oke — literally "top cloth" in Yoruba — is a hand-loomed fabric with centuries of history in southwestern Nigeria, traditionally woven in narrow strips and sewn together into wider cloth. It's not printed or machine-woven the way most fabric is; the pattern is built into the weave itself, thread by thread, on a loom. That's why real aso-oke has a texture and structure you can feel — a slight ridge, a woven-in sheen — that no printed imitation replicates.

It's traditionally reserved for significant occasions — weddings, chieftaincy ceremonies, milestone celebrations — which is part of why it carries the weight it does. Wearing it is a statement about the occasion's importance, not just a fabric choice.

The Weave Types Worth Knowing

SanyanWoven from wild silk, naturally beige-brown. The most prestigious and traditionally the most expensive — historically reserved for royalty and titled elders.
AlaariWoven with a rich red base, often threaded with metallic accents. Bold, ceremonial, built to be the focal point of a room.
EtuDeep indigo, almost black. Traditionally associated with maturity and quiet authority rather than spectacle.

Beyond the traditional three, contemporary aso-oke now comes in an enormous range of colourways and metallic-thread combinations — which is part of what makes matching it to aso-ebi or a specific event colour scheme so achievable today.

Why Aso-Oke Specifically Calls for Custom Work

Ready-to-wear aso-oke pieces exist, but the fabric's structure makes them a poor fit for standard sizing in a way lighter fabrics aren't. Aso-oke doesn't drape or stretch the way cotton or linen does — it holds its shape, which means a cut that doesn't account for your actual proportions shows immediately, especially around the shoulders and waist. A custom pattern, built to your measurements from the start, is less a luxury here than a practical requirement for the fabric to sit the way it's meant to.

There's also the matter of pairing — aso-oke is traditionally styled with a gele (headwrap), and sometimes an ipele (shoulder sash) or buba, all cut from the same cloth. Getting the proportions right across multiple pieces is exactly the kind of detail a made-to-measure process handles and off-the-rack simply can't.

How the Custom Process Actually Works

  • Choose your garment and fabric. Dress, blouse, trousers, a full co-ord set, or an evening gown — paired with your aso-oke colourway or another fabric of your choice.
  • Specify the details. Neckline, sleeve length, silhouette, and any embellishment — embroidery, beading, lining.
  • Submit your measurements — bust, waist, hip, and length, with a guide if you're unsure how to take them yourself.
  • We confirm and begin. A member of our team calls to finalise every detail before cutting starts, so nothing is assumed.
  • Delivery in 10–14 business days, or 5–7 with rush delivery if your event is closer than that.
